AI policy templates

A one-page starting point, not a finished policy. The valuable part is the never list, and that part has to be yours.

The structure every version uses

  1. Approved tools. Which tools, on which account, paid for by whom.
  2. Never goes in. Five specific categories in your own words.
  3. Check before it leaves. What gets reviewed, by whom, on what.
  4. Disclose. To clients, funders, insurers or a court — where applicable.
  5. Ask. A named person, not “management”.
  6. Reviewed. A date, and a trigger for re-reading it.

Four starting variants

ForWhat's different about it
Professional practice (accounting, legal, conveyancing)Heavier on verification and client confidentiality terms; explicit exclusion of advice and tax positions.
Clinic (allied health, dental, veterinary)Privacy-first framing, de-identification method attached, reception and clinician rules separated.
NDIS or care providerWritten for a shift-based casual workforce, attached to induction, with audit evidence in mind.
Field business (trades, cleaning, maintenance)Short, phone-first, task-framed. Sign-off and certification explicitly unchanged.

How to use one

Take the closest variant, rewrite the never list with the words your business actually uses for the things it holds, name a person, date it, and attach it to induction. That's a real policy. A generic one nobody rewrote is decoration.

Not legal advice

These are operational starting points. Where a disclosure duty, an insurer's requirement or a regulator's practice note is in play, that's a question for your adviser.
