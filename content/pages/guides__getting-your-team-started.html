Getting your team started

Four weeks, in order. The order is the part that matters — most businesses do this backwards and then wonder why nothing stuck.

Week one: decide the account, not the strategy

Pick one tool and one tier, and pay for it. Nearly every problem in this area starts with staff on personal free accounts, and no policy survives contact with a team that has to buy its own tools.

Which tool depends on the shape of your work — the mapping is here. Which tier depends on what you hold, which is the more important question.

Week two: write one page

Which tools are approved, what never goes in, what gets checked before it leaves, what must be disclosed, who to ask. One page, in your words, at the reading level of your newest casual.

There are templates here. The work is in the never list, because that part has to be specific to your business to mean anything.

Week three: pick three jobs

Not a strategy. Three documents you produce constantly that nobody enjoys writing: the client update, the quote, the report, the chasing email. Boring is correct here — frequency is what makes the return.

Week four: teach the method, then build the setups

Briefing properly, judging output, rejecting the first draft, verifying against the source. Then build the three setups so the method has somewhere to live, with real examples in them.

What failure looks like

An enthusiastic afternoon, a shared document of prompts, and nothing in use three weeks later. The two things that prevent it are a funded account and setups that exist rather than being planned.

What to do about what's already happening

Assume some of your team are already using these tools, because they almost certainly are. Asking without consequence attached produces honest answers and a much better starting point than a ban you can't enforce.

See what this looks like for your business

Nine questions, about four minutes, and a plan ordered for your own work. No account, and the full plan is shown before any email is asked for.
