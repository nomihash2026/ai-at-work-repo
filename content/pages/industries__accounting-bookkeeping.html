Where the line sits

Client financial records do not go into a general-purpose tool until the tool, the tier and the written rule are settled. The reframe that matters: most of the work that eats the week involves no client data at all.

The boundary we teach

No tax positions, no advice, no client identifying data in a general-purpose tool.

The objection is always that client data is confidential, and it is. The answer is that the work eating your week is mostly drafting, chasing, restructuring and explaining — tasks that never need a client's name in them. The training draws that line explicitly before anything else is taught.

How it's usually delivered

For accounting & bookkeeping the usual starting shape is Half-day team workshop. The assessment confirms it against your own answers rather than assuming.

Who's in the room. Partners and managers buy. Graduates and client managers are the audience in the room.

What you'd have at the end

  • A first-draft client explanation the manager only has to correct rather than write
  • One chasing sequence built in the room and used that week
  • A workpaper narrative pattern the whole team writes to

The questions we get asked

Our client data is confidential.

It is, and it stays out. The tasks in the list above are drafting, chasing, restructuring and reading — none of them need a client identified. We settle that boundary in writing before the team touches a tool.

Won't it invent a tax position?

It will, confidently, which is exactly why tax positions are on the never list. The training is about the writing and reading around the technical work, not the technical judgement.

My team will use it to avoid learning.

That is a real risk with juniors and it gets addressed directly — the rule taught is that you have to be able to defend the output, so the reviewer's job doesn't get harder.

Regulatory context

Relevant bodies and frameworks: CPA Australia, Chartered Accountants ANZ, Institute of Public Accountants, Tax Practitioners Board. CPD obligations exist across all three professional bodies and the TPB. Hour counts deliberately not asserted here.

We name no CPD hour figures anywhere on this site. Requirements differ by body and by state, and an out-of-date number is worse than none.
