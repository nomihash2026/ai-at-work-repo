Claude for long documents

The method matters more than the tool, but this is the tool we teach it on.

Reviewed September 2026. This page names fast-moving vendor detail, so it carries a review date.

The method

  1. State what the document is and what you're deciding. Reading for a settlement date is a different read from reading for risk.
  2. Ask named questions rather than asking for a summary.
  3. Require a location for every claim, so verification takes seconds.
  4. Ask what's missing, ambiguous or inconsistent — often the most valuable output.
  5. Spot-check two answers by hand. Always.

What it's good at, concretely

  • Finding every place an obligation appears, including the one on page 84.
  • Comparing two versions of a document and reporting what materially changed.
  • Turning a long document into the four questions you should ask about it.
  • Restructuring your own long notes into a document with a spine.

Where it goes wrong

  • Very large inputs get handled in pieces, and the answer still arrives confident.
  • Scanned or badly formatted documents lose structure, especially tables.
  • Asked to interpret rather than locate, it will produce something that reads like advice.

The professional line

Locating, comparing and structuring is the work. Interpretation stays with the practitioner, and in regulated settings that boundary goes in the written policy rather than in someone's judgement.
