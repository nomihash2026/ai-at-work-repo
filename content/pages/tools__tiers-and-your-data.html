Tiers and what happens to your data

The fastest-moving content on this site, and the most important. Treat the principles as durable and every specific as checkable.

Reviewed September 2026. This page names fast-moving vendor detail, so it carries a review date.

Why the tier is the whole question

Vendors have historically treated consumer free tiers differently from paid and business tiers, particularly on whether content may be used to improve models, how long it's retained, and who inside the company can review it. That is a different risk profile, not a different price.

Which matters because unofficial workplace use happens almost entirely on personal free accounts. A clinic can have an excellent privacy policy and six staff on consumer accounts at the same time.

How to read this page

We deliberately don't reproduce vendor terms here, because they change and a stale copy is worse than none. The durable part is the set of questions to ask and the decision to write down. The current answers get confirmed against the vendor's own terms at the start of every engagement.

The five things to establish, per tool and per tier

  1. Is content used to train or improve models, and can that be turned off?
  2. How long is it retained, and what happens on deletion?
  3. Who can review it, and under what circumstances?
  4. Where is it processed and stored, and does that matter for your obligations?
  5. Is there an administrator who can see, export or control staff activity?

What to do with the answers

  • Pick one approved tool and tier, and say so in writing.
  • Provide the account rather than expecting staff to buy it. Unfunded policies get ignored.
  • Name the tasks that are allowed on that account, and the ones that aren't allowed anywhere.
  • Re-check when the vendor changes terms, and date the document.

The vendor-agnostic version

The same five questions work for Copilot, Gemini, a transcription service or a practice management vendor adding “AI features” — questions to ask any AI vendor.
