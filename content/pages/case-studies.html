What engagements look like

No named case studies published yet. Rather than invent composites, here's the honest shape of each engagement and what changes as a result.

A half-day workshop, start to finish

Morning, six to twelve people, a room with a screen. Twenty minutes on the boundary. An hour correcting our draft of your task list, which is the part that earns trust. Then method on real documents, then three or four setups built live with your own examples in them. Named owners and a review date before anyone leaves.

What changes: the documents you produce most often get a first draft you correct rather than write, and the team agrees on what good looks like. What doesn't: anything that required a rule you haven't written.

A workflow build, start to finish

One job — usually same-day quoting or the monthly report. Built from your existing documents, run on live work together, fixed where it breaks. Handover is the setup, a one-page how-to, and the follow-ups already written.

What changes: that one job stops being a Sunday. Nothing else changes, deliberately.

A policy and audit engagement, start to finish

A non-disciplinary look at what's already happening — which tools, which accounts, which tasks, which vendors in your stack have quietly added AI. Then a one-page rule in your words, at the reading level of your newest casual, mapped to your obligations.

What changes: you know your actual exposure rather than your assumed exposure, and there's a rule to train against. Frequently the audit is the part people describe as worth the money.

Why there are no named studies yet

Client work in accounting, legal, health and NDIS settings is confidential by default. Anything published here will be with written consent and with the specifics the client agreed to, which takes longer than writing a composite but is the only version worth reading.
