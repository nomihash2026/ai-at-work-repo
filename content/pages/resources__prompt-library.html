Starting prompt library

Deliberately a small library. A hundred prompts is a worse resource than six you understand well enough to change.

Why prompt lists usually disappoint

A prompt written for someone else's business produces output for someone else's business. What transfers is the structure: role, context, audience, examples, constraints, and what to refuse.

The six patterns worth having

The recurring client update

Context on the matter or job, the audience's knowledge level, what changed, what happens next, and the tone your business actually uses.

The chasing sequence

Three escalating requests for the same missing thing, polite throughout, each usable without the previous one being read.

The long-document question set

Named questions rather than “summarise”, with a requirement to cite a location for each answer.

The notes-to-report pattern

De-identified notes in, a structure the recipient expects out, with gaps flagged rather than filled in.

The quote or scope

Your inclusions, your exclusions, your terms, and the sentence that prevents the argument in month three.

The complaint response

Acknowledge, state what happened, state what changes, and nothing else. Written from facts you supply, not invented.

Turning a pattern into something reusable

A prompt you retype is a prompt you’ll stop using. Once one works, it belongs in a held setup — reusable setups covers that, with the tool-specific mechanics under Projects and custom setups.

Before you paste in an example

Examples are the most powerful part of any brief and the easiest place to leak client information. De-identify them once, properly, and reuse the clean versions.
