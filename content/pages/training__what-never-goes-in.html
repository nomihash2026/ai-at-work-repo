What never goes in

Every business needs a short list of things that never go into a general-purpose AI tool. Short, specific and written down, because a vague rule is the same as no rule.

Why “be careful” fails

“Use your judgement with confidential information” gives a receptionist under pressure at 4:55pm nothing to work with. A list of five specific things they must never paste in does, and it takes about twenty minutes to write.

The categories that are almost always on it

  • Identifiable records about the people you serve — patients, clients, participants, tenants.
  • Anything that identifies a person indirectly: rare conditions, small-town detail, combinations that narrow to one household.
  • Credentials, access codes and account details, including in screenshots.
  • Material you hold under someone else's confidentiality terms, which is often tighter than privacy law.
  • Work where a wrong output is a regulated harm: clinical decisions, advice, safety sign-off, tax positions.

De-identification that survives a bad day

A rule only works if it's faster than breaking it. The version that holds up is a habit: roles instead of names, ranges instead of dates, the detail that makes the document useful without the detail that makes it identifiable. We practise it on the business's own documents until it's automatic.

Assume it's already happening

In most businesses we look at, someone is already pasting things in on a free account. The useful response is a rule and a tier decision, not an investigation — and the audit part of an engagement is designed to find out without turning into a disciplinary process.

Where this sits

The never list is the personal-behaviour half. The organisational half is your team's AI policy, and the vendor half is tiers and what happens to your data. All three are short documents.
