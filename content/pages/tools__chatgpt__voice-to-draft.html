The problem it solves

The quote gets written at 8pm, or Thursday, or never. Job notes get reconstructed from memory three days later. Variations don't get written up, so they don't get billed. None of this is a discipline problem — it's that writing takes a desk and the day doesn't have one.

How it actually goes

  1. Talk for ninety seconds about the job: what you saw, what it needs, roughly what's involved.
  2. A held setup turns that into your quote format, with your inclusions and exclusions and your terms.
  3. You read it, fix the number, send it. Same day, from the driveway.

Why it needs a setup, not just dictation

Raw transcription of a voice note is a mess and reads like one. The value is in the setup holding your format, your standard inclusions and the phrases you always use — which is why this module and reusable setups are taught together.

What to leave out of the recording

Customer names and addresses aren't needed for the drafting and shouldn't be in it. “The unit in the second building” works fine, and keeps the whole workflow clear of personal information.

The follow-up half

Most trade and field businesses never follow up a quote. Two follow-ups, written once and reused, is usually the cheapest revenue available — and it's built in the same session.
