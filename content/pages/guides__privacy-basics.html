Privacy basics for small business

Not a legal treatment. The practical version: what changes when a chat window enters a business that holds information about other people.

Reviewed September 2026. This page names fast-moving vendor detail, so it carries a review date.

The shape of the obligation

If you hold personal information about people — patients, clients, participants, tenants, employees — you have obligations about how it's collected, used, disclosed and secured. The Australian Privacy Principles under the Privacy Act are the framework, and health service providers are covered regardless of turnover.

Pasting a client's record into a third-party tool is potentially a disclosure and potentially an offshore one. That's the whole issue in a sentence, and it's why the tier question matters more than the tool question.

Where this page stops

This is orientation, not advice. Thresholds, exemptions and notifiable breach duties turn on specifics, and health, NDIS and legal practice each carry obligations on top of the Privacy Act. Get your own advice before relying on any of it.

The four decisions that do the work

  1. One approved tool and tier, funded by the business. Everything else depends on this.
  2. A written never list. Specific categories, not “be careful”.
  3. A de-identification habit. Roles instead of names, ranges instead of dates, practised until automatic.
  4. A record of the decision. Dated, so you can show what you decided and when the tools changed.

The indirect identification trap

Removing a name isn't always de-identification. “The 34-year-old woman with the rare condition at the Wagga clinic” identifies someone to anybody who knows the practice. Small communities and unusual circumstances collapse anonymity quickly, and this is the part people get wrong in good faith.

Your existing software counts too

Practice management systems, transcription services and CRMs are all adding AI features, often on by default. The same questions apply — questions to ask any AI vendor.

Where to read the source

The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner publishes the APP guidelines and guidance for small business. Read the primary source rather than a summary of it, including this one.
