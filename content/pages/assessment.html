What the plan actually gives you

Three things. The training modules in the order that suits your work rather than the order they're listed in. A reading on where the business is exposed right now, based on what you've told us about tools, records and whether a written rule exists. And one recommended starting point, which is sometimes a workshop and sometimes a policy engagement instead.

Where the answer is that training shouldn't be the first thing you do, it says so. A team trained to move faster in a direction nobody has decided on is a worse position than an untrained one.

Who should fill it in

Whoever signs off on training and carries the risk if it goes wrong — owner, principal, practice manager, director. If that isn't you, finish it anyway and send it on; the plan is written to be forwarded.

What happens to your answers

The whole thing runs in your browser. Nothing is transmitted while you answer, and the full plan is shown before any email address is asked for. If you choose to send it through, we use it to reply and to prepare a scope — nothing else. Details on the privacy page.

If your industry isn't in the list

There's a general path that produces a real plan without a published track, because about seventy per cent of this work is identical across industries. The foundations don't change; the examples do.
