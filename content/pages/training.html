One curriculum, six foundations

The same core skill set underpins every industry track. The order changes, the examples are yours, the foundations don't move.

Why one spine and not twelve courses

Run the task lists for an accounting practice, a physio clinic and an electrical contractor side by side and about seventy per cent of the entries are the same job wearing different clothes: explain something in writing, read something too long, turn mess into structure, say the same thing consistently forty times, and know when to stop.

So the foundations are written once and taught properly. What your industry track adds is vocabulary, the deadline that actually hurts, and the compliance line you can't cross. That's a thin layer on a solid base, which is the opposite of how most AI training is built.

How the modules get ordered

Ordering is the part that matters. A clinic with no written rule and clinicians already pasting notes into a free account does not need module one; it needs the policy and the never list, and everything else afterwards. A trade business with no compliance exposure and forty unfollowed quotes needs reusable setups on day one.

The assessment does that ordering from nine answers. It will also tell you when training is not the first thing you need, which is a genuine outcome and not a sales move.
