Half-day team workshop

Everyone to the same standard in one go, on your documents, with the setups built while the room is still there.

What happens

  1. The boundary first. What never goes in, for your business specifically, and the tier decision. Twenty minutes, and it changes how everything else is heard.
  2. Your task list. We arrive with a draft of the work that eats your week and correct it with the room. This is where people stop being polite.
  3. Method on real documents. Briefing, judging output, rejecting the first draft, verifying against the source.
  4. Setups built live. Three or four for the documents you produce most, with your own examples in them.
  5. Who owns what. Named owners, a review date, and what to do when it drifts.

Who's in the room

Whoever produces the documents. In most practices that's a mixed room of senior and junior staff, which works well — the seniors supply the standard and the juniors supply the honest account of what's actually being done now.

Some industries split. Real estate sales and property management barely overlap, so they run as separate sessions; a combined room means half the people are bored for half the time.

What you have at the end of the day

  • Three or four working setups on your most common documents.
  • A one-page written rule covering tools, tier and the never list.
  • A team that agrees on what good output looks like here.
  • A short list of what to try next, in order.

When this isn't the right first step

If staff are already using AI on client material with no written rule, a workshop makes that faster rather than safer. The policy and audit work comes first, and the workshop lands properly once it exists.
