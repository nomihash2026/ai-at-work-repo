Why we teach two tools

It costs about twenty per cent more time and it's the main reason the training is still useful in two years.

One-tool training has a structural problem

A course built around a single product has a quiet incentive to describe that product's weaknesses as acceptable and to avoid the comparison that would expose them. It becomes a sales channel whether or not anyone intended it to.

Four concrete reasons

  1. Data handling differs. Between vendors, and between tiers of the same vendor. For anyone holding client records that's the first question, and you can only answer it honestly by comparing.
  2. The shapes of work genuinely differ. Long careful reading and high-volume quick turnaround pull toward different tools. A rule of thumb about that beats a prompt list.
  3. Your team is already split. Most staff have ChatGPT on their phones; some workplaces standardise on something else entirely. Training one tool leaves half the room out.
  4. The interface is the disposable part. If you can brief a task, judge an output and tell where your data goes, the next product is a menu layout.

What tool-neutral means here, specifically

  • No reseller, partner or affiliate relationship with either vendor.
  • No referral fee in any direction.
  • The free tier gets recommended where it's genuinely sufficient.
  • Where one tool is clearly better for your work, we say which and why, and you can disagree with the reasoning.
