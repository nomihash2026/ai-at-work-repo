What never goes in an AI tool

Every business needs this list. Five categories, specific to you, written down. It takes about twenty minutes and it's the highest-value twenty minutes available.

The five categories

  1. Identifiable records about the people you serve. Names, records, case detail, anything that would identify someone to a person who knows them.
  2. Credentials and access. Passwords, keys, codes, account numbers — including inside screenshots, which is how it usually happens.
  3. Material held under someone else's terms. Client confidentiality clauses are often tighter than privacy law and rarely read before the tool arrives.
  4. Work where a wrong output is a regulated harm. Clinical decisions, advice, tax positions, safety sign-off, award interpretation.
  5. Anything you couldn't check. If nobody in the business can verify the output, it shouldn't be produced this way.

Write yours like this

Take the five above and make each one concrete for your business, using your own words for the things you hold. “No participant progress notes with names in them” is a usable rule. “Protect sensitive data” is not.

De-identifying without wrecking the document

  • Roles instead of names: the tenant, the participant, the referrer.
  • Ranges instead of dates and ages where precision isn't doing work.
  • Drop the geography when the geography narrows it to one person.
  • Keep the clinical, technical or factual detail — that's the part making the output useful.

The test

Would someone who knows your business be able to tell who this is? If yes, it isn't de-identified, regardless of whether the name is in there.

What to do when it's already gone in

It happens, usually on a personal free account, usually with good intentions. The useful response is to find out the extent, check whether your notifiable breach obligations are engaged, fix the account and tier problem, and write the rule. Treating it as a disciplinary matter first reliably produces incomplete information.
