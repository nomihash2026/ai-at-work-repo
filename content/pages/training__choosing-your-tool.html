Choosing your tool for the job

Feature comparisons go out of date in weeks. A rule of thumb about the shape of the work lasts, because the shapes don't change as fast as the products.

Why we don't teach one tool

Most of the market teaches ChatGPT only, which quietly turns the training into a sales channel for one vendor. It also leaves people helpless the moment their workplace standardises on something else, which happens more often than anyone predicted.

Teaching two properly costs about twenty per cent more time and produces a skill that transfers. It also makes the data-handling conversation honest, because you can compare rather than assert.

The rule of thumb

Shape of the workReach for
Long input, one careful answer — a bundle, a contract, a ruling, a plan setClaude
Sustained drafting that has to sound like you across many documentsClaude
Reasoning through something with several moving partsClaude
High volume, fast turnaround, twenty small jobs in an hourChatGPT
Talking instead of typing — voice note to finished draftChatGPT
Photo of a whiteboard, a form, a site, a labelChatGPT
Anything where a wrong answer is a regulated harm, or identifiable records are requiredNeither

The part people skip

Both vendors treat your information differently depending on which tier you are on, and the free tiers are where most unofficial workplace use happens. That's not a footnote to tool choice, it's the main event for anyone holding client records — tiers and what happens to your data.
