Where the line sits

Nothing identifiable about a patient goes into a general-purpose AI tool. Not the name, not the record, not the detail that would identify them to someone who knows them. That line is drawn in writing before anyone is taught a single prompt.

The boundary we teach

No clinical decisions, no diagnosis, no identifiable patient information in a general-purpose tool.

In allied health the privacy module is the product, not the disclaimer. Lead with where the line sits and what each vendor and tier does with what you type, because a principal who trusts you on that will hear everything else. A principal who doesn't, won't — and shouldn't.

The family-level rule for health & care: Nothing identifiable about a patient, client or participant goes into a general-purpose AI tool. Not the name, not the record, not the detail that would identify them to someone who knows them.

How it's usually delivered

For allied health clinics the usual starting shape is Privacy and policy session, then the workshop. The assessment confirms it against your own answers rather than assuming.

Who's in the room. Owner or principal buys. Clinicians and reception are the audience, and they have different rules.

What you'd have at the end

  • A written, one-page AI rule the whole clinic can follow
  • A de-identification habit that survives a busy Thursday
  • Referrer letters drafted from de-identified notes in minutes

The questions we get asked

Isn't this a privacy breach waiting to happen?

It is, if nobody has drawn the line. That's why the line comes first: which tool, which tier, which records, written down, before any productivity content.

My clinicians are already pasting notes in.

Assume they are. That's the reason to move now, and the audit part of the engagement is designed to find out without turning it into a disciplinary process.

We can't afford clinician time out of the diary.

Which is why the first session is short and aimed at the people who set the rules, not the whole clinic.

Regulatory context

Relevant bodies and frameworks: AHPRA and the National Boards, Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, Profession-specific associations. Privacy content on this vertical should be reviewed by a qualified privacy adviser before publication. This is the one vertical where being confidently wrong does real damage.

We name no CPD hour figures anywhere on this site. Requirements differ by body and by state, and an out-of-date number is worse than none.
