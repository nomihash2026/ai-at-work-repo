AI policy & audit

Your staff are probably already using these tools. The principal's real question isn't what productivity is available — it's how exposed the business is right now.

The audit half

A short, deliberately non-disciplinary look at what's actually happening: which tools, which accounts, which tasks, what's being pasted in. Done as a fact-finding exercise rather than an investigation, because the version where people feel accused produces unreliable answers.

  • Which tools and tiers are in use, including personal accounts.
  • Which tasks they're being used on, and which of those touch client material.
  • What vendors in your existing software stack have quietly added AI features.
  • Where your current obligations and your current practice don't match.

The policy half

One page, in your words, that answers which tools, which tier, which data, what must be checked, what must be disclosed, and who to ask. Written so the newest casual can follow it, not just the operations manager.

Where this is the right first engagement

  • Law firms and conveyancers, where staff use is usually unreported and the exposure question comes before any training.
  • Allied health clinics, where the privacy line is the product and trust on it unlocks everything else.
  • NDIS providers, where documentation and policy is both the biggest time sink and the first thing an auditor reads.

Not legal advice

We write the operational rule and map it to your obligations. Where a question is properly legal — a specific disclosure duty, an insurer's requirement, a jurisdiction's practice note — it goes to your adviser, and we'll tell you when we've hit that line.

What follows

Usually the training, which lands very differently once there's a written rule to teach against. Sometimes nothing for a quarter, which is a fine outcome.
