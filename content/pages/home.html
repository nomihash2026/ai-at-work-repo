Two tools, taught side by side

Most courses teach one tool and quietly become a sales channel for it. We teach Claude and ChatGPT together, because the useful skill is knowing which one to reach for and what each does with your information.

Where your data goes differs between vendors and between tiers. That difference is the first thing a practice principal needs answered, so it's the first thing we cover — not a disclaimer at the end.

Claude

And when to use neither

Why the industry page matters more than the course

The tasks people need AI training for are roughly the same everywhere: drafting, summarising long documents, structuring messy information, client communication, reusable setups, and knowing when not to use it at all. What differs is the vocabulary, the deadline that hurts, and the line you cannot cross without a problem.

So there is one core skill set, written once, taught properly — and a thin application layer per industry that makes it land. A practice owner should read three lines of their industry page and recognise their own Tuesday. If they don't, that page isn't ready and we haven't published it.

That also means we say no to some work. Anything where a wrong output is a regulated harm rather than an inconvenience — clinical decisions, financial advice, tax positions, safety sign-off — stays with the qualified person, and the training says so out loud.
