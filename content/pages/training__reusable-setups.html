Building reusable setups

This is the module that decides whether anything survives the week after the training. Everything else is technique; this is the part that sticks.

The pattern that fails

Someone learns to write a good prompt, gets a good result, and then types it again from memory a week later, badly. Two weeks on they've stopped. Nothing was wrong with the technique — it just had nowhere to live.

What a setup holds

  • Who you are, what the business does, and who the audience usually is.
  • Your examples — the past documents that define the voice and structure.
  • The standing rules: what's never mentioned, what's always included, how long, what tone.
  • The boundary: what this setup must refuse to do.

In Claude this is a Project. In ChatGPT it's custom instructions or a saved setup. The mechanics differ, the content is the same, and the content is the valuable part — it ports to whatever replaces both.

The three or four worth building

Nobody needs twenty. Most businesses have three or four documents that account for the bulk of the writing, and they're usually boring: the client update, the quote, the report, the chasing email. Build those, properly, with real examples in them.

Built in the room, not afterwards

In a workshop these get built while everyone is there, on the business's own documents. A setup that exists at 4pm on the day gets used. One in someone's notes to build later, doesn't.

Keeping them honest

Setups drift. The examples age, a rule changes, someone edits it and doesn't tell anyone. Two habits fix it: one named owner per setup, and a quick read every quarter against a recent real document.
