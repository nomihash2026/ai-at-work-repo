Where the line sits

Site addresses, access codes and staff details stay out of it. Nothing in the drafting work needs them.

The boundary we teach

Safety sign-off, chemical handling determinations and licensing stay with the accountable person.

Contracts are won on the submission and kept on the monthly report. Both are writing jobs, and both are done late at night by the owner. Everything else in the business is instruction quality across a casual workforce, which is also writing.

The family-level rule for trades & field services: Customer names and addresses can stay out of it entirely. A quote does not need them to be written in four minutes instead of forty.

How it's usually delivered

For cleaning & property maintenance the usual starting shape is Done-with-you workflow build. The assessment confirms it against your own answers rather than assuming.

The questions we get asked

My staff barely read the instructions now.

That's usually a writing problem rather than a reading one, and it's the cheapest thing on this list to fix.

Tenders are too specific for AI.

The specific parts are yours. The structure, the repeated content and the compliance schedule are not, and they're most of the document.

We're too busy to change anything.

Then we build one thing — usually the monthly report or the quote — and leave the rest alone.

Regulatory context

Relevant bodies and frameworks: Safe Work Australia and state WHS regulators, Fair Work Ombudsman, Cleaning industry award conditions. Award interpretation and WHS determinations are not delegated to a general-purpose tool. Wage and award questions go to the relevant authority or an adviser.

We name no CPD hour figures anywhere on this site. Requirements differ by body and by state, and an out-of-date number is worse than none.
